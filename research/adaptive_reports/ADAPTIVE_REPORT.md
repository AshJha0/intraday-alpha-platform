## READ THIS FIRST — what two sessions can and cannot show

**The sample is two synthetic sessions (6.5 h of equity flow and about
21 h of FX quotes each).** That is far too short to conclude that any
refit policy improves live P&L:

- **SCHEDULED(weekly) cannot fire even once** — the sample crosses no
  week boundary, so it is *identical to STATIC here by construction*.
  Its column exists to prove the machinery, not to rank it.
- SCHEDULED(daily) fires exactly once (the day-2 boundary), so the
  comparison rests on a single refit on a single synthetic day.
- Drift baselines come from a 2.5-hour warmup of the same generator —
  real regime shifts are not in the data, so PSI stays mostly below
  threshold and most drift-triggered refits come from the rolling-IC
  z-score, which is noisy at this sample size.
- P&L differences between policies below are **within noise**; do not
  read a ranking into them. Every alpha is net-negative after costs
  under every policy on this data (0 of 40 deployments positive),
  as the promotion report shows.

What the study DOES establish: the adaptability machinery is
deterministic and leak-free (asserted + shift-tested), refits trigger
exactly when the pinned rules say, retirement verifiably halts
allocation, every transition is logged with a reason, and every look
at the data is counted in the experiments ledger.

## Pinned configuration (configs/strategies/strategies.json `adaptive`)

- blocks 0.25h, warmup 2.5h, trailing train window 24h, embargo 60s
- drift trigger: PSI > 0.25 (10-quantile-bucket, eps 1e-6) OR rolling-IC z < -2.0; min refit gap 1h
- lifecycle: WATCH below IC 0.0, RETIRE on a breach once the CUSUM of the shortfall below the gate (slack 0.0025, each reading weighted by the 0.125 of its window that is new) reaches 0.01, re-activate at IC >= 0.005 for 3 consecutive evals
- rolling-IC z: two-sample HAC z of the pair-weighted rolling IC against the baseline (`ic_z_method = hac`)
- backtest: the `v2` research rules (cost-aware positions, fills capped at displayed size, only the rows the IC scores, square-root impact); every P&L figure is in USD

### Which policy 'wins' where — and why that is not a conclusion

| policy | best net P&L (of 10) | best deployed IC (of 10) |
|--------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| static | 8 | 5 |
| scheduled_weekly | 0 | 0 |
| scheduled_daily | 1 | 3 |
| drift_triggered | 1 | 2 |

Ties go to the first policy in table order; static and
scheduled_weekly are bitwise identical here (weekly never fires), so
wins credited to `static` are shared with `scheduled_weekly` by
construction. With one synthetic day of true out-of-warmup data these
win counts are coin flips, not evidence. The honest headline: **on
this sample, refitting neither rescues nor ruins any alpha — the
differences are one to two orders of magnitude smaller than the cost
drag.** A real ranking needs months of sessions.

## Drift monitor readout

PSI is computed per evaluation against warmup baselines for the
alpha's signal and each declared feature; KS (D, p) is diagnostic
only. Counts of evaluations with any PSI above the pinned trigger
threshold (0.25) appear in the master table
(`drift ev`). Baselines are serialized to `research/baselines/` in
the /API_ADAPTIVE.md schema (x-version 2: each file records the
`feature_version` it was captured against, and a loader rejects a
baseline from a different feature registry) — the same files the
Java live monitor consumes.

**No OOS IC baseline: FX10.** The IC baseline is
captured from the purged held-out tail of the warmup; when that
tail yields too few 5-minute IC buckets, no baseline is written
and the rolling-IC gauge is UNAVAILABLE for that alpha. Read the
consequence: for these alphas every drift event and refit below
comes from PSI alone, and the lifecycle gauge never sees an IC
reading, so they cannot be retired on decay. This is reported
rather than papered over with an in-sample baseline — round 2
used the warmup model's own training rows, which biased `ic_z`
negative and fired refits on the IS/OOS gap instead of on drift.

**Known false-positive pattern (reported, not hidden):** FX10
monitors `minute_of_day_v1`, and a time-of-day feature 'drifts' by
construction as the session progresses — its PSI against a
morning-warmup baseline exceeds the threshold at most evaluations
(hence its large drift-event count and refit count under the
drift-triggered policy). This is exactly the class of monitor
misconfiguration a production deployment must catch in review:
deterministic calendar features do not belong in a drift trigger.
The machinery behaves as pinned; the lesson is about monitor
selection and is left visible here on purpose.

## Multiple testing

Multiple testing: 4396 experiments recorded. Bonferroni per-test p-threshold 1.14e-05 (|t| >= 4.39); deflated-Sharpe-style selection yardstick: expected max |t| under the global null is ~4.10 — any t below that is consistent with pure selection over this many trials.

One deployment (alpha x refit policy) is ONE experiment in
`research/experiments.json` (kind `adaptive_deployment`), and the
ledger de-duplicates reruns of the same configuration. Counting each
monitoring evaluation as an experiment — as earlier versions did —
made the selection-adjusted threshold a function of the block cadence
rather than of the research design.

The ledger held **4396** recorded evaluations in 208 distinct configurations when this report was rendered. Pipelines that run after it add theirs: the current total is in `research/experiments.json`.
