## How to read these notes (for every reader)

**The plain idea.** Each sentence that says something is true about a strategy, a dataset or the platform carries a
label saying how strong the support is. The labels go from strongest to weakest; most of this release sits at the weak
end, and the notes put the negative results first so they cannot be missed.

**The formal statement.** The seven evidence classes of U0 section 10, exactly:

| class | meaning | what the manifest must cite |
|---|---|---|
| independently confirmed | a registered single-shot decision executed under custody separate from the developer | signed prereg, runner log, runner record, custody with a separate approver |
| mechanically isolated | the same, executed by the registered runner under the developer's sole custody | signed prereg, runner log, runner record |
| development-supported | supported on development or validation data, ledgered, never on sealed data | at least one artefact |
| exploratory | a look that suggested something; nothing is claimed from it | at least one artefact |
| rejected | tested and failed, registered or not | the registered set, or artefacts |
| data-ineligible | cannot be tested on the data the programme holds | a reason and an artefact |
| pending | not yet decided | a reason; no evidence cited |

While the owner alone holds development, approval and sealed credentials, no claim in v2.0.0 can be **independently
confirmed**; the strongest available class is **mechanically isolated** (U0 section 2 item 6). The builder refuses an
"independently confirmed" claim whose custody names the developer as approver.

**A worked example.** A family registered on Track B under PF-1 and run once by the runner, where Holm rejected nothing,
appears in section 1 as: "D1 short-term reversal: no hypothesis promoted on Track B. **rejected** (prereg
`research/preregs/<<id>>.json`, runner seq <<n>>, result `<<sha12>>`)." The same family, had it promoted, would appear
in section 5 as **mechanically isolated**, never higher.
