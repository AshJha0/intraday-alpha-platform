# Microprice and Queue Dynamics as Short-Horizon FX Predictors: A Negative Result with a Regime-Conditioned Exception

> Dated record. The figures below are those of the dataset in force when the paper was written; the 2026-10-03 update restates them on the v1.4.0 dataset and re-checks each conclusion, and the 2026-10-04 update at the end restates them under the v1.5.0 default research methods (same dataset) and re-checks each conclusion again.

*Intraday Alpha Platform research series, paper 2 of 6 (spec §28). Generated 2026-08-29 from the repository's committed research artifacts.*

---

## Abstract

We evaluate microprice deviation and quote/queue microstructure features as
short-horizon predictors on the platform's synthetic multi-venue FX dataset,
using flagship alphas FX01 (quote/microprice imbalance, 500 ms horizon) and
FX09 (volatility-regime reversion, 1 m horizon). The headline is a
disciplined negative result: FX01 is REJECTED with a pooled out-of-sample IC
of -0.0150 (Newey-West t = -0.30) and fold sign consistency of only 1/4 —
the microprice, a reliable equity-book predictor on the same platform (EQ01:
IC 0.0219, t = 3.81, sign-stable and hypothesis-confirmed, ITERATE — though,
like every alpha on this dataset, cost-negative at -42,746), carries no
stable signal on the synthetic FX quote book at its designed horizon. By contrast, FX09
posts the strongest FX statistics in the study (IC 0.1131, t = 14.3, hit
rate 0.563, IC 0.176 in high-volatility states), but with two honesty
flags: its fitted sign contradicts the stated economic rationale
(hypothesis-confirmation gate fails), and it loses -670,712 currency units
net at 1x costs, so it too cannot be promoted. Regime conditioning is the
one robust structural finding: FX09's edge is ~60% larger in
high-volatility states, and FX01's anti-signal concentrates there almost
entirely. Queue-dynamics features in the
literal equity sense (FIFO depletion/replenishment) are shown to be
structurally unavailable on a quote-driven L1 FX book — a data-model
limitation we state explicitly rather than paper over. All data is
synthetic.

## 1. Introduction

The microprice — the depth-weighted expected next mid,
`(Pb*Qa + Pa*Qb)/(Qb+Qa)` — and the dynamics of the queues behind it are the
workhorse short-horizon predictors of the market-making literature. The
platform computes both as pinned features
(`python/src/iap/features/microstructure.py`: `microprice_v1`,
`micro_mid_dev_bps_v1`) and tests them in both asset classes. This paper
reports the FX side: FX01 applies microprice deviation to the consolidated
multi-LP quote book, and FX09 conditions a reversion signal on the
volatility regime. We deliberately include the equity microprice result
(EQ01) as a controlled contrast, because the *same feature, same pipeline,
same validation* yields opposite verdicts across the two book models.

## 2. Data

**All data is synthetic**, generated by
`python/src/iap/marketdata/generator.py` with a pinned SplitMix64 seed: two
days (2026-08-24/25) of FX QUOTE + TRADE streams for 8 synthetic G10 pairs
(instrument ids 101-108) across three liquidity providers (venues LP1, LP2,
PRI). Each pair has one shared regime-switching mid; venues differ by
quote spread and receive-timestamp latency. A QUOTE event replaces the
venue's entire side at L1 — the FX book is quote-driven, not order-driven.

Volumes (`data/normalized/qc_report.json`): 49,668 / 49,557 FX events on
days 1/2 within a 310,159-event normalized dataset; QC found 322 sequence
gaps, 450 duplicates, 182 out-of-order and 173 invalid events across all 46
streams. Feature frames: instrument 101 has 6,573 event-time emissions with
a median inter-emission gap of ~14.9 s (computed from
`data/features/features_101.parquet`), an order of magnitude sparser than
the equity streams — which bounds how fast an "FX microstructure" signal can
be sampled at all on this dataset.

### The queue-dynamics caveat, stated up front

The platform's queue features (`queue_depletion_rate_*`,
`queue_replenish_rate_*`, used by equity alpha EQ05 in
`python/src/iap/alpha/equity.py`) require observing FIFO order flow at a
price level. A quote-driven L1 book has no observable queue: each QUOTE
atomically replaces the side. "Queue dynamics" for FX in this study
therefore means the observable proxies — L1 depth imbalance
(`imbalance_l1_v1`), quoted depth, and microprice deviation — and we say so
rather than implying MBO-grade queue inference.

## 3. Methodology

Identical to paper 1 (spec §13, §20): 4 expanding walk-forward folds with
purge and embargo, pooled OOS IC / Rank IC / Newey-West t, automated
shift-by-one leakage test, cost stress {0.5, 1, 2}x, latency stress
{+0, +1, +5} events, and a volatility-regime IC split
(`python/src/iap/validation/stress.py`). The FX cost model charges spread +
2.5-per-million commission + linear impact
(`configs/execution/execution.json`). Signal definitions
(`python/src/iap/alpha/fx.py`):

- **FX01** (`fx_quote_imbalance`): `micro_mid_dev_bps_v1` on the
  consolidated FX book, fitted linear scaling, 500 ms horizon.
- **FX09** (`fx_vol_regime_reversion`):
  `-ret_vol_adj_10s_v1 * vol_regime_ratio_v1` — negative vol-adjusted
  10 s return amplified by the ratio of 1 m to 5 m realized vol, 1 m horizon.

The multiple-testing ledger stands at 1,224 recorded experiments
(`research/alpha_reports/REPORT.md`): Bonferroni |t| ≥ 4.10, expected max
|t| under the global null ≈ 3.77.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Headline statistics

From `research/alpha_reports/REPORT.md` and the per-alpha JSONs
(`FX01.json`, `FX09.json`, `EQ01.json`):

| alpha | book model | horizon | OOS IC | Rank IC | NW t | hit | folds+ | hyp | net P&L (1x) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FX01 | FX quote (L1) | 500ms | -0.0150 | 0.0047 | -0.30 | 0.529 | 1/4 | yes | -24,283 | REJECT |
| FX09 | FX quote (L1) | 1m | 0.1131 | 0.1318 | 14.33 | 0.563 | 4/4 | **no** | -670,712 | ITERATE |
| EQ01 | equity MBO | 1s | 0.0219 | 0.0419 | 3.81 | 0.576 | 2/2 | yes | -42,746 | ITERATE |

FX01's per-fold ICs are -0.0240, -0.0294, +0.0034, -0.0187 (`FX01.json`,
`folds`): the sign is not merely weak, it is unstable, and the pooled point
estimate is *negative* — the opposite of the microprice hypothesis. The
same microprice feature on the equity MBO book is significant, sign-stable
across its (two non-degenerate) test folds, and hypothesis-confirmed — it
fails only the universal cost gate, at a modest 53 flips/hour.

### 4.2 Decay profiles

Last-fold OOS IC by horizon (REPORT.md decay table; FX has no 10 ms bucket —
quotes are too sparse):

| alpha | 50ms | 100ms | 500ms | 1s | 5s | 10s | 30s | 1m | 5m | 15m |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FX01 | -0.001 | +0.002 | -0.010 | +0.005 | +0.014 | +0.009 | +0.030 | +0.026 | -0.010 | -0.013 |
| FX09 | +0.032 | +0.015 | +0.015 | +0.035 | +0.050 | +0.072 | +0.117 | +0.140 | +0.109 | +0.052 |

FX01 is noise at its own designed horizon (-0.010 at 500 ms) with a faint
positive ridge at 30 s-1 m that does not survive scrutiny (see §4.4). FX09
shows a coherent hump peaking exactly at its pinned 1 m horizon (0.140),
decaying on both sides — the shape one expects of a genuine
conditional-reversion effect on this mean-reverting generator.

### 4.3 Regime conditioning: the one structural positive

Volatility-regime IC split, last fold (REPORT.md; flag =
`vol_regime_flag_v1`):

| alpha | high-vol IC | low-vol IC |
|---|---|---|
| FX01 | -0.0508 | +0.0048 |
| FX09 | +0.1762 | +0.1095 |
| EQ01 | +0.0186 | +0.0243 |

Both FX alphas are regime-dependent, in instructive ways (the equity
control EQ01 is not — its IC is similar in both regimes). FX09's edge grows
~60% in high-vol states, consistent with its overreaction rationale. FX01's
*wrongness* also concentrates in high-vol states (-0.051): on this
generator, elevated volatility makes LP quote skews anti-predictive — thin,
wide quotes move the size-weighted microprice around without any
corresponding drift in the shared mid.

### 4.4 Why FX09 still cannot be promoted

Three separate gates stop FX09 (`FX09.json`):

1. **Hypothesis sign** (`hypothesis_confirmed: false`): the fitted slope
   contradicts the documented rationale's sign. Under the platform's rules
   (REPORT.md: "alphas with hyp=no can never be PROMOTE regardless of
   |IC|"), a right answer for an unverified reason is not promotable.
2. **Costs**: net P&L at 1x is -670,712 (x0.5: -322,438; x2: -1,367,259).
   The day-2 backtest grosses +84,925 but pays 1,742,033 in costs across
   5,483 trades. At 66 flips/hour on FX commission and spread, gross IC of
   0.113 is nowhere near enough.
3. **Leakage-adjacent caution**: the shift-by-one IC only drops from 0.1414
   to 0.1138 (`FX09.json`, `leakage`) — the test passes, but the signal is
   slow-moving, so most of it survives a one-event lag; it is a 1-minute
   reversion effect, not a microstructure edge.

### 4.5 Latency sensitivity

From the stress blocks (`FX01.json`, `FX09.json`):

| shift | FX01 IC | FX09 IC |
|---|---|---|
| +0 events | -0.0187 | 0.1414 |
| +1 event | -0.0075 | 0.1138 |
| +5 events | -0.0005 | 0.0345 |

FX01's "signal" attenuates towards zero under lag exactly as noise does.
FX09 retains 80% of IC after one event (~15 s median) — further evidence it
is a slow conditional-reversion effect for which latency investment is
irrelevant (see paper 4).

### 4.6 Cost stress and the held-out day

Cost stress (last fold, net P&L) and day-2 out-of-sample backtest with
day-1-fitted parameters (REPORT.md):

| alpha | x0.5 costs | x1 costs | x2 costs | day-2 gross | day-2 costs | day-2 net | day-2 trades |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FX01 | -12,306 | -24,283 | -48,235 | -4,905 | 69,024 | -73,929 | 1,900 |
| FX09 | -322,438 | -670,712 | -1,367,259 | +84,925 | 1,742,033 | -1,657,108 | 5,483 |

FX09's day-2 gross alpha is genuinely positive out-of-sample (+84,925) —
the conditional-reversion prediction transfers to the unseen day — and is
then buried twenty-fold by commission and spread at 5,483 trades. FX01 is
negative even gross. No cost multiplier in the stress grid changes either
verdict.

### 4.7 Corroboration from the ML study

The platform's independent ML run (`research/ml_reports/ML_REPORT.md`)
reaches the same destination from the other direction: across 51 curated
predictors (microstructure features included) and six model classes, the
honest directional measure — IC against the mid-to-mid label — is ~0 to
slightly negative for every model on this dataset, and the report
concludes "no exploitable 5s directional signal" exists in the curated
feature set. The single-feature FX results above are therefore not a
failure of linear modeling that a tree or MLP rescues.

## 5. Limitations

1. **Synthetic data, and structurally so.** The FX generator drives all
   venues of a pair from one shared mid with venue-specific spread noise
   (`python/src/iap/marketdata/generator.py`). A microprice computed over
   quotes whose sizes are not chosen by informed LPs has no reason to lead
   the mid — the FX01 null is partly *built into the data model*, and we
   flag that rather than presenting it as evidence about real FX. The
   equity contrast (EQ01) is meaningful precisely because the equity
   generator maintains real FIFO books whose depth responds to flow.
2. **No true queue observability in FX.** Stated in §2; queue-dynamics
   conclusions transfer to equities only (EQ05: IC 0.0116, t = 3.96, also
   cost-negative at -474,288 — REPORT.md).
3. **Sparse sampling.** ~6.6k emissions per pair over two days; sub-second
   FX horizons have few valid label pairs (FX01's 500 ms bucket exists but
   is thin: `n_ic_buckets` 263, `FX01.json`).
4. **FX09's t = 14.3 overstates certainty.** With 1,224 ledger experiments and
   heavily overlapping 1 m labels on two days of data, sampling error is
   understated; the fold-consistency evidence (4/4, ICs 0.105-0.141) is the
   more defensible statistic.
5. **Cost model.** Taker-style costs on every flip; a market-making
   implementation of a quote-book signal would face a different (and
   unmodeled) economics of queue priority and adverse selection — see
   paper 5.

## 6. Conclusions

On this platform's synthetic FX dataset, the microprice is not a
short-horizon FX predictor: FX01 fails with an unstable, pooled-negative IC
at its designed 500 ms horizon and is rejected outright, while the identical
feature on the equity MBO book is significant, sign-stable and
hypothesis-confirmed (failing only the cost gate that fails all 24 alphas
here). The instructive general point is that *microprice predictability
is a property of the book mechanism, not of the formula* — where displayed
depth reflects genuine queued commitment (MBO), its imbalance carries
information; where it is an LP's replaceable quote (L1 quote-driven), it
need not.

The regime-conditioned reversion alpha FX09 delivers the largest FX IC in
the study (0.113 pooled, 0.176 in high-vol) and survives every purely
statistical gate, yet is correctly held at ITERATE: its fitted sign
disagrees with its stated rationale and it is catastrophically cost-negative
at 1x. Both results demonstrate the framework doing its job — separating
"predicts the label" from "is understood" and from "makes money," and
refusing promotion unless all three hold (spec §13, §20, §32).

## Erratum / Update — 2026-09-06 (round-3 research fixes)

1. **The FX headline statistics were a stale-LP artefact.** ~30 % of the
   consolidated FX rows in this dataset are CROSSED: one LP's quote is stale
   inside the aggregation window, the merged mid is not a tradable price, and
   it reverts mechanically when that LP refreshes. A vol-scaled momentum or
   reversion signal is paid for measuring exactly that reversion. Conditioning
   the IC on the book state (the reports now always do):

   | alpha | pooled IC | uncrossed IC | crossed IC | crossed frac |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | FX01 | -0.0153 | 0.0183 | -0.0302 | 0.287 |
   | FX08 | 0.1167 | 0.0409 | 0.1922 | 0.318 |
   | FX09 | -0.1284 | -0.0472 | -0.2100 | 0.317 |
   | FX10 | 0.0674 | 0.0198 | 0.1151 | 0.318 |
   | FX11 | 0.0667 | 0.0329 | 0.1061 | 0.316 |

   The claim that FX09 carries "the strongest FX statistics in the study" is
   **withdrawn**: its uncrossed Newey-West t is
   -3.81 (against a selection yardstick of
   3.64), and the PROMOTE gate — which now reads
   the uncrossed IC — puts it at REJECT.
2. **FX01's sub-second REJECT was mis-explained.** (FX01 is no longer a
   REJECT at all — see "Erratum to the errata" below; the explanation
   correction in this item stands, the verdict word in its heading does
   not.) §5 attributed it to "few
   valid label pairs at sub-second horizons". The label VALIDITY is high; the
   labels are *exactly zero* in 98-99 % of rows at 500 ms-1 s because the
   median inter-emission gap on this generator is ~15-22 s. That is a
   sampling artefact of the data, not evidence against microprice in FX.
   `data/features/features_summary.json` now publishes
   `label_zero_frac_by_horizon` and REPORT.md prints it above the master
   table; horizon choice should be gated on P(label == 0).
3. **Labels no longer span halts, stale-venue gaps or a frozen mid**, and the
   forward mid must be fresher than `max(5 s, 2 x median quote gap)` — on the
   FX book that is ~33 s, so the 1 m label validity is now ~74 %, not ~100 %.
4. **Walk-forward folds** are quantiles of the row index; degenerate folds are
   reported and count as failures (see paper 01's erratum item 1).
5. **Execution**: latency is 1 s of EVENT time with a 60 s decision-age bound
   and session flattening; every P&L figure above is superseded by the current
   REPORT.md.

The multiple-testing ledger was **reset and re-derived** for this round: an
experiment is now identified by (alpha, kind, canonical configuration) and
re-running a script no longer increases the count, and one adaptive
deployment counts as ONE experiment instead of its 211 monitoring
evaluations. Every "1,224 experiments" / "19,347 experiments" figure in the
body above is superseded by **760 experiments
(65 distinct configurations)**: Bonferroni
per-test |t| **3.99**, expected max |t| under
the global null **3.64**. Reports now read the
ledger at render time, so a report and the ledger can never disagree again.
